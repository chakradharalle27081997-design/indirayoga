Why Regular Physical Activity Is Essential for a Healthy Life

Discover how consistent movement supports strength, mobility, heart health and everyday well-being.

By NaviraYoga · 24 November 2026 · 2 min read

Share on WhatsApp ↗

Movement is part of everyday health

Fitness is not limited to athletes or people trying to lose weight. Walking to a shop, climbing stairs, carrying groceries and getting up from a chair all depend on physical capacity. Regular activity helps maintain that capacity and can support independence as we age. A routine that fits ordinary life is often more valuable than occasional intense workouts.

Different activities serve different purposes

Aerobic activity challenges the heart and lungs. Brisk walking, cycling or swimming are examples. Strength training challenges muscles and supports everyday tasks. Yoga can contribute to balance, mobility and muscular control, depending on the practice. These activities complement one another rather than competing for a single definition of fitness.

What the guidelines recommend

The WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity per week for adults, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. These are population-level targets, not a requirement to begin at full volume. Some activity is better than none, and people with health limitations may need adapted goals.

Break up long sitting periods

A person who exercises before work may still spend most of the day seated. Short movement breaks can help vary posture and reduce uninterrupted sitting. Stand up, walk a short distance or perform comfortable ankle movements between tasks. These breaks complement planned exercise rather than replacing it.

Make the first step manageable

If you have been inactive, begin with a short comfortable walk and a few supported movements. Increase duration gradually and allow recovery. Seek medical advice before unfamiliar vigorous exercise if you have relevant health concerns. Stop and seek urgent assessment for chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness during activity.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to exercise for an hour every day?

No. Activity can be accumulated in shorter sessions. Build a routine appropriate to your health and schedule.
