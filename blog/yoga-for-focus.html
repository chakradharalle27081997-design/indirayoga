How Regular Yoga Can Help Improve Focus and Mental Clarity

Build attention through gentle movement, breath awareness and realistic work breaks.

By NaviraYoga · 24 November 2026 · 2 min read

Share on WhatsApp ↗

Attention is a skill you can practise

A busy day often involves switching between messages, meetings and unfinished tasks. When you finally sit down to concentrate, your attention may continue moving between them. Yoga offers an opportunity to practise noticing where attention has gone and returning to a chosen activity. It is not a guarantee of improved productivity, but the process can be useful in everyday life.

Connect movement and attention

During a simple standing posture, notice the pressure under your feet and the position of your shoulders. When your mind moves to a work problem, return to the physical sensation without criticizing yourself. This is a practical exercise in redirecting attention. You do not need to eliminate thoughts to practise successfully.

What evidence suggests

Research on yoga and mindfulness suggests possible benefits for stress and some aspects of cognitive function, but studies differ in quality and methods. It is not established that a particular posture will improve concentration or memory for everyone. Sleep, workload, health and the environment also influence attention.

A ten-minute transition before work

Spend two minutes settling in a chair, three minutes on comfortable shoulder and ankle movements, three minutes noticing sensations during supported standing and two minutes deciding on your first work task. Keep the phone aside during this period. If quiet breath awareness is uncomfortable, focus on an external object or the contact of your feet with the floor.

Bring the practice into your day

Before switching tasks, finish or write down the next step for the current task. Close unnecessary tabs and choose a realistic period of uninterrupted work. A brief movement break between demanding tasks can help you change pace. Yoga is most useful when it complements practical changes to the conditions that make concentration difficult.

Frequently asked questions

Will yoga treat attention disorders?

No. Persistent attention difficulties deserve appropriate assessment; yoga can be an optional supportive practice.
