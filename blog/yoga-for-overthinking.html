Overthinking? 5 Mindful Yoga and Breathing Practices to Help You Unwind

Five gentle ways to pause and relate differently to repetitive thoughts.

By NaviraYoga · 24 November 2026 · 2 min read

Share on WhatsApp ↗

When thoughts keep returning

Replaying a conversation or worrying about tomorrow can make it difficult to rest. Trying to force thoughts away may create further frustration. A gentle practice can offer a different approach: notice what is happening and redirect attention to something concrete. The aim is not to empty the mind or guarantee that worries disappear.

1. Ground through your feet

Sit on a stable chair and notice the contact of both feet with the floor. Observe pressure, temperature or the texture beneath your feet. If attention returns to a worry, gently notice the physical sensation again. Keep your eyes open if that feels more comfortable.

2. Notice ordinary breathing

Observe the movement of your breath without deliberately deepening it. Continue for a minute or two if comfortable. If breath awareness increases anxiety, switch to noticing sounds or objects around you. There is no requirement to focus on breathing.

3. Release unnecessary effort

Make a few slow shoulder rolls and let your hands rest. Notice whether you are clenching your jaw or raising your shoulders. Avoid forcing the neck or stretching into pain. The movement is an invitation to change posture, not a test of relaxation.

4. Choose supported rest

Sit with back support or lie comfortably with pillows. Allow a few minutes without a task to complete. If stillness feels uncomfortable, try a gentle walk instead. Different people respond differently to quiet practices.

5. Write down one next step

If a worry concerns a practical problem, write down the concern and one action you can take at an appropriate time. If no action is possible now, acknowledge that uncertainty rather than repeatedly searching for an answer. Return to an ordinary activity afterward.

When extra support matters

Yoga may help some people manage everyday stress, but persistent intrusive thoughts, anxiety or sleep disruption may require professional mental health support. You do not need to wait until symptoms become severe to ask for help.

Frequently asked questions

Can yoga stop overthinking permanently?

No. It may help you relate differently to thoughts, but it is not a guaranteed treatment.
